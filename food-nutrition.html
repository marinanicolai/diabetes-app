Good food is medicine. Discover diabetes-friendly recipes, meal planning strategies, and nutrition tips backed by science.

Eating Well with Diabetes

There's no one-size-fits-all "diabetes diet" — but these principles will steer you right.

🥦 Fill Half Your Plate with Vegetables

Non-starchy vegetables like leafy greens, broccoli, peppers, and zucchini are low in carbohydrates and high in fiber, vitamins, and minerals. Aim for variety and color.

🍗 Choose Lean Proteins

Protein helps you feel full and doesn't spike blood sugar. Good options include skinless chicken, fish, tofu, beans, lentils, eggs, and low-fat dairy.

🌾 Pick Whole Grains

Whole grains like brown rice, quinoa, oats, and whole wheat bread have more fiber and cause a slower rise in blood sugar than refined grains.

🥑 Healthy Fats Matter

Focus on unsaturated fats from avocados, nuts, seeds, and olive oil. Limit saturated fats from red meat and full-fat dairy. Avoid trans fats entirely.

The Diabetes Plate Method

The Diabetes Plate Method

The Diabetes Plate Method is an easy way to plan healthy meals without counting carbs or weighing food. Just use a 9-inch plate and divide it as shown.

  • ½ PLATE Non-starchy vegetables
  • ¼ PLATE Lean protein
  • ¼ PLATE Whole grains or starchy vegetables

Add a glass of water or zero-calorie drink, and a small serving of fruit or dairy on the side.
