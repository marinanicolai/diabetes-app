Advocacy

We fight for fair policies, affordable insulin, and equal rights for every person with diabetes. Add your voice to the movement.

Why Advocacy Matters

Despite major advances in diabetes science, millions of Americans still struggle to afford the insulin and supplies they need to survive. Policy change is essential to make diabetes care accessible and equitable.

The ADA's advocacy team works every day at federal and state levels to fight discrimination, expand insurance coverage, and cap the cost of insulin. But we need your voice to make it happen.

Policy Questions Answered

The ADA has been the leading advocacy voice on insulin affordability for years. We championed the $35/month insulin cap for Medicare beneficiaries and continue to push for an equivalent cap for all Americans with commercial insurance. We've also worked state by state to pass emergency insulin access laws in over 24 states.

The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, and other federal laws protect people with diabetes from discrimination in employment, school, and other settings. The American Diabetes Association provides free legal resources and, in some cases, legal advocacy through our Legal Advocacy Program.

You can start by signing up for our advocacy alerts to stay informed about key issues and take action when it matters most. You can also contact your elected officials, share your personal diabetes story, participate in our annual Call to Congress event, or become a trained community advocate through our Advocate Network program.

The ADA supports healthcare coverage for all people with diabetes, including affordable access to insulin, CGMs, insulin pumps, diabetes self-management education, and medical nutrition therapy. We believe access to diabetes care should not depend on where you live, how much you earn, or what insurance you carry.
